# Report 139: MinZ against SDCC 4.6 on the frozen C/Nanz benchmark (2026-11-25)

On 26 small frozen C cases, MinZ at `9daf7a8ba` needs fewer Z80 T-states than SDCC 4.6.0 on the
geometric mean (0.629 of SDCC's speed profile), and about the same number of bytes (0.981). The
spread per case is large: SDCC is faster than MinZ's default profile on 5 of the 26 cases and
smaller than MinZ's size profile on 9. The set was chosen by the MinZ project; this report does not
claim that MinZ generates better code than SDCC in general.

All numbers below come from [`c89-benchmark-2026-11-25/`](c89-benchmark-2026-11-25/README.md): the
raw rows (`all-summary.json`, `fusion-summary.json`), `geomean.py`/`geomean.txt`, and
`tables.py`, which prints every table in this report (`python3 tables.py . > tables.md`).

## Method

- **Harness:** `research/c89-benchmark/run.py` ([README](../research/c89-benchmark/README.md)).
  Each sample starts a fresh flat 64 KB Z80 emulator with the linked image and explicit global
  inputs, calls `execute()` and counts T-states through its `RET`, including helpers. Results,
  specified global memory and PC/SP are checked for every sample; MinZ and Nanz rows also replay on
  the raw and the final MIR VM.
- **Two runs** at `9daf7a8ba`: `--suite all --include-nanz` (12 main + 5 heldout C cases, 6 Nanz
  ports, 1 tuple variant) and `--suite fusion` (9 iterator-fusion C cases with idiomatic Nanz and
  hand-written Z80). 220 rows, all `PASS`. Exact commands are in the data
  [README](c89-benchmark-2026-11-25/README.md).
- **Metrics:** `TotalT` is the T-state total of a case's whole frozen workload; `ProgramBytes` is
  occupied image bytes plus declared data storage (not file length, not stack).
- **Ratio:** MinZ / SDCC per case; the headline is the geometric mean over the cases both sides
  have. Below 1 means MinZ needs less.
- **Source identity:** every row's `SourceSHA256` matches a file under `research/c89-benchmark/`
  at `9daf7a8ba`.

73 of 208 comparisons (26 cases x 2 MinZ C profiles x 2 SDCC profiles x 2 metrics) favour SDCC: 22 on T-states, 51 on bytes.

MinZ C default vs SDCC speed, T-states: SDCC strictly better in 5 of 26 cases (checksum16, fusion_map_filter_sum, fusion_map_store, fusion_two_ptr_xor, in_interval).

The largest T-state losses are `fusion_two_ptr_xor` (1.635× SDCC speed), `in_interval` (1.338×),
`fusion_map_filter_sum` (1.146×) and `checksum16` (1.117×). In bytes, `in_interval` is the
largest loss apart from the table in `fusion_dot_product` (50 against 40 bytes in the default
profile, 43 in the size profile); every other byte loss is between 1.003× and 1.065×.

## Limits

- **Small, chosen set.** 26 C cases (12 main, 5 heldout, 9 fusion), each a single kernel without
  libc, I/O or startup code. The cases and their workloads were written by the MinZ project, and
  much of the November optimisation work was measured on the main and fusion suites. The heldout
  suite is the only part not used to choose optimisations (see the heldout discipline in the
  harness README).
- **No general claim.** A geometric mean under 1 on this set says nothing about programs outside
  it. Per case, SDCC wins 22 of 104 T-state comparisons and 51 of 104 byte comparisons.
- **SDCC is used as shipped**, with its two documented profiles and no extra flags beyond those in
  the harness. Other SDCC options, other compilers (sccz80, z88dk, llvm-z80) and SDCC 4.5 are not in
  this run.
- **Emulated timing.** T-states are counted by the harness emulator on a flat 64 KB machine, with
  no contended memory, interrupts or wait states.
- **One run, one commit.** The numbers describe `9daf7a8ba` only. Earlier reports in `reports/`
  (2026-10-16 to 2026-10-22) used other compiler trees and SDCC 4.2/4.5/4.6 and are not
  comparable row by row.
